Figure 1. The finite-difference trap on a noisy analog cost. The transverse-field program H(θ)=θZ0+X0 is evaluated Hamiltonian-level under the T4 noise model (dephasing, T/T2*=0.5); the cost C_noisy(θ)=⟨Z0⟩ (black) develops sharp θ-features. At the operating point (dot), either sound differentiation strategy of Sec. 4 — kick-PSR or the Nyquist waveform shift — recovers the true noisy slope (blue tangent). Finite differences do not: secants at three step sizes ε (orange) all return the WRONG sign, and shrinking ε does not rescue them — the δ/ε control-noise floor (Sec. 6.2) blows up as ε→0. No step size works.

DATA NOTE (Fig 1): H(θ)=θ·Z0+X0, cost C_noisy=⟨Z0⟩ under T2* dephasing, T/T2*=0.50 (Hamiltonian-level under T4). Anchor θ*=1.235. Slope verification (R2): drawn tangent slope = analytic ∇C_noisy = -1.240 (central difference h=1e-3) — EQUAL by construction (the tangent is plotted with exactly this slope); coarse np.gradient check on the plotted samples gives -1.33, consistent to grid resolution. Three FD secants at ε=[0.15, 0.25, 0.35]: slopes +0.09/+0.96/+0.66 — all POSITIVE while ∇C_noisy is NEGATIVE (all three wrong sign). θ*=1.235 is the steepest point at which all three flip (on this sharp landscape that pins it just past a crest); tangent drawn over ±0.06 so it hugs the descending flank. Caveat: the smallest ε=0.15 secant is barely wrong-sign (slope +0.09, near-flat).
