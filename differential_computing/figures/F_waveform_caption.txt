Figure (App F). The running example as it is actually emitted, both
differentiation lanes, on the device's six physical channels.
H(x) = sin(2x)(Z0Z1 + X0 + X1) at x = 0.7, T = 5.0 us, two qubits, compiled
through the same pipeline as Figure 5 (diffQCProvider -> TweezerMapper ->
physical_channels -> PulseDSL -> AWG sample arrays); rows are |A(t)| except the
two transport rows, which show the AOD tone frequency mapped to atom position
(the tones are constant-amplitude, so their envelope carries no information).

The two lanes are drawn side by side, EACH ON ITS OWN TIME AXIS, because the NSR
branch is 24x shorter and on a shared axis its structure collapses into a sliver
at the origin. The strip beneath the columns is the one place the two are
commensurable: it carries both branch durations on a single scale, and it is
what the 24x should be read off. The ripple on the addressing rows is physical,
not sampling noise -- the comb carries one tone per atom, 10 MHz apart, so |A|
beats with a ~100 ns period; the dressing AOM is a single global tone and is
flat.

PSR (kick): the inserted CZ is a digital operation in the gate zone, so the
schedule must transport the pair 100 um and back around it. Transport plus gate
plus transport occupy 114.1 us of the 119.8 us branch -- 95% of the wall clock
for 5 us of semantic evolution time.

NSR (waveform shift): the same frozen geometry and the same single evolution
segment; the transport AODs and the gate AOM are silent, and the branch differs
from the source only in the amplitudes on the addressing and dressing channels
(x 0.985 for the drawn Nyquist mode n = 0, s = 0.2067 at bandwidth K = 1.210).
The insets resolve the carrier: the measured 696 ns gate shape, and the
addressing Rabi comb, where the shifted branch reproduces the source carrier and
phase exactly and rescales only the amplitude.

What the figure does NOT claim: that NSR wins overall. It compares ONE branch of
each strategy. A gradient needs many branches either way -- PSR spends them on
the tau quadrature, NSR on drawn shifts -- and how many each needs is the (p, q)
question Figure 10 answers. What is settled here is the per-branch sequence: the
shift rule's branch is the source program's own schedule, 24x shorter, with the
transport AODs and the gate AOM never keyed.

The shifted branch is exact, not approximate: the running example's three terms
share one coefficient, so H(x+s) = [sin(2(x+s))/sin(2x)] H(x), and every machine
instruction is linear in its amplitude variable at frozen geometry. Scaling the
amplitudes therefore realizes the shifted target with residual 8.8e-5, exactly
the source compile's 9.0e-5 residual times the scale factor -- no solve, no
re-mapping, and no additional compilation error.

Time-axis note: unlike Figure 5, which simplifies the moves to direct
single-leg hops on an event-spaced axis, this figure uses the module's realistic
transit lanes (transit_dy = 5 um: lift, travel, drop) and a to-scale axis, so
the two figures' timelines are not numerically identical by construction.
