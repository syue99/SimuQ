Figure (App F). The running example as it is actually emitted, both
differentiation strategies, on the device's six physical channels.
H(x) = sin(2x)(Z0Z1 + X0 + X1) at x = 0.7, T = 5.0 us, two qubits, compiled
through the same pipeline as Figure 5 (diffQCProvider -> TweezerMapper ->
physical_channels -> PulseDSL -> AWG sample arrays). Both branches come out of
ONE compile, so the two lanes share a geometry and a solve.

Each row is the compiled record itself, Re w(t), not an envelope. A ~100 MHz
carrier cannot be resolved across 120 us of page, so every row is drawn the way
an instrument draws a record too dense to resolve: the minimum and maximum of
the real samples in each display bin, filled between. The band's outline is the
true excursion of the signal; nothing is smoothed or interpolated. The three
panels underneath resolve the carrier from the same schedules at 0.5 ns -- the
transport tone mid-move, the addressing comb, and the measured gate pulse -- so
the bands above can be read as the oscillations they are.

On the two transport rows the black line is the atom position, on the right-hand
axis: the same tone through the device's fixed linear AOD calibration,
f = 100 MHz + (0.05 MHz/um) x. The x row is the pair chirped out to the gate zone
about 100 um away and back; the y row is the transit lane, with its notch where
the atoms are dropped for the gate.

The trap tone is on in both lanes. It is a standing condition of the tweezers
rather than a scheduled operation, so the compiler emits transport entries only
where atoms MOVE, and the transport rows are the one place the record is
reconstructed rather than read from the sample array: the tone frequency comes
from the real schedule where it exists and holds at the atom's position
elsewhere. A silent AOD would mean a dropped register, which is why the NSR lane
shows a held tone rather than silence there.

The difference the figure is about is STRUCTURAL, not a duration. The PSR branch
inserts a CZ, a digital operation in the gate zone, so the schedule must chirp
the transport AODs and fire the gate AOM to move the pair there and back. The NSR
branch is the source program's own schedule: the AODs only hold, the gate AOM is
never fired, and the branch differs from the source only in the amplitudes on the
addressing and dressing channels (x 0.985 for the drawn Nyquist mode n = 0,
s = 0.2067 at bandwidth K = 1.210).

No speedup is claimed from the per-branch durations. On a real machine the branch
itself is not the operative cost -- measurement and atom loading / rearrangement
dominate the shot budget. What the figure settles is which channels each strategy
has to drive at all.

The shifted branch is exact, not approximate: the running example's three terms
share one coefficient, so H(x+s) = [sin(2(x+s))/sin(2x)] H(x), and every machine
instruction is linear in its amplitude variable at frozen geometry. Scaling the
amplitudes realizes the shifted target with residual 8.8e-5, exactly the source
compile's 9.0e-5 residual times the scale factor -- no solve, no re-mapping, and
no additional compilation error.
