Figure (App F). The running example as it is actually emitted, both
differentiation strategies, on the device's six physical channels.
H(x) = sin(2x)(Z0Z1 + X0 + X1) at x = 0.7, T = 5.0 us, two qubits, compiled
through the same pipeline as Figure 5 (diffQCProvider -> TweezerMapper ->
physical_channels -> PulseDSL -> AWG sample arrays). Both branches come out of
ONE compile, so the two lanes share a geometry and a solve.

Every row is a quantity the hardware emits. The four drive and gate rows show
|A(t)| of the compiled sample arrays. The two transport rows show the
SYNTHESIZED TONE FREQUENCY: those channels play constant-amplitude tones, so
amplitude carries no information there and frequency is what the synthesizer
varies. One line per atom -- the AOD comb assigns one tone to each -- so the x
row is the pair being chirped from the interaction zone out to the gate zone and
back, and the y row is the transit lane they ride, with its notch at the gate.
Atom position is this same trace through the device's fixed linear AOD
calibration, f = 100 MHz + (0.05 MHz/um) x, and is kept in the cache; it is a
derived quantity and is deliberately not what the rows plot. The ripple on the
addressing rows is the comb beat (one tone per atom, 10 MHz apart), not sampling
noise. The columns are on their own time axes, labelled with their spans, and
the column widths carry the duration difference.

The difference the figure is about is STRUCTURAL, not a duration. The PSR branch
inserts a CZ, which is a digital operation in the gate zone, so the schedule must
key the transport AODs and the gate AOM to move the pair there and back. The NSR
branch is the source program's own schedule: the transport AODs and the gate AOM
are never keyed, and the branch differs from the source only in the amplitudes on
the addressing and dressing channels (x 0.985 for the drawn Nyquist mode n = 0,
s = 0.2067 at bandwidth K = 1.210).

No speedup is claimed from the per-branch durations. On a real machine the
branch itself is not the operative cost -- measurement and atom loading /
rearrangement dominate the shot budget, and the microsecond-scale difference
between these two branches does not decide anything. What the figure settles is
which channels each strategy has to touch at all, and therefore which parts of
the machine its branch depends on.

The shifted branch is exact, not approximate: the running example's three terms
share one coefficient, so H(x+s) = [sin(2(x+s))/sin(2x)] H(x), and every machine
instruction is linear in its amplitude variable at frozen geometry. Scaling the
amplitudes therefore realizes the shifted target with residual 8.8e-5, exactly
the source compile's 9.0e-5 residual times the scale factor -- no solve, no
re-mapping, and no additional compilation error.

Time-axis note: unlike Figure 5, which simplifies the moves to direct single-leg
hops on an event-spaced axis, this figure uses the module's realistic transit
lanes (transit_dy = 5 um: lift, travel, drop), so the two figures' timelines are
not numerically identical by construction.
